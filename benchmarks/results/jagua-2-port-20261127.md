# Engine port to jagua-rs 2.0.0 — 2026-11-27

Development evidence for moving Nestbolt from jagua-rs 1.1.0 and Sparrow
`4629b227` to jagua-rs 2.0.0 and Sparrow `73c02f65`. No held-out job was run
and no benchmark claim follows: the published v1 results stay tied to build
`75cbb32`, and the Sparrow comparator in the benchmark scripts keeps its pin.

## Result

At 60 seconds over three development seeds, the ported build used less
material than `main` on 40 of 65 jobs and more on 20 (three-seed medians). The
median change was -0.10% and the mean -0.37%. All 390 layouts validated.

## Speed

In 20-second runs the port made a median 2.30 times as many separator
evaluations per real second as `main` (1.27 to 3.59 over the 58 jobs with
separator work). The first construction of the 1,000-part job took 1.38 s
against 4.14 s.

## What changed

| | `main` `4ac32f71` | Port |
| --- | --- | --- |
| jagua-rs | 1.1.0 | 2.0.0 (crates.io) |
| Sparrow | `4629b227` | `73c02f65` |
| Work-clock version | `nestbolt-work-v4` | `nestbolt-work-v5` |
| Release binary SHA-256 | `eec649ac…489f` | `87045a2e…ab81` |

The compile changes are mechanical: `CDEConfig` lost `quadtree_depth` and
`cd_threshold`, and `SPSurrogateConfig` lost `n_ff_piers` (always 0 here).
The quadtree depth tuned for the browser in H4 no longer exists. Sparrow's
split of its command line into a separate crate takes the lock file from 217
packages to 190.

### Collision margins on 2.0.0

jagua-rs 2.0.0 removes every vertex closer than 1e-5 of a polygon's diameter
to the previous one, in every polygon it builds. That includes its own
inflated and deflated collision outlines. With a separation near the
floating-point guard, a corner's round join collapses and one end of the next
edge lies on the original outline. An L-shape 4 units across, imported with
separation 2.9e-5, comes back with six vertices and its top edge on the
original.

On the port with the 1.1.0 separation, the independent validator rejected
constructed trial layouts that 1.1.0 never produced:

| 80 passes per seed, seeds 1000-1005 | jagua-rs 1.1.0 | 2.0.0, old separation | 2.0.0, new separation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `b-blaz1-nest-mb-given`, rejected trials per seed | 0 | 1 to 7 | 0 |
| `b-blaz1-nest-mb-free` | not completed (a 1.1.0 debug assertion) | 1 to 8 | 0 |
| `b-fu-nest-mb-free`, `-given` | 0 | 1 in six seeds | 0 |
| 18 academic jobs, 8,640 trials | not run | not run | **0** |

Each rejected layout overlapped by about 4e-6 of a part's area. No invalid
layout was returned in any run.

`engine_separation` now sizes the separation so that the margin Nestbolt needs
survives the removal: twice the shortest allowed edge for part outlines, and
the loss at corner arcs for sheet walls and end blockers. Jobs with drawn stock
outlines or defects take the general bound at sheet scale. New tests measure
the margin jagua keeps for parts, walls, blockers and a remnant outline; three
of the four fail with the 1.1.0 formula.

| Job | Separation before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1.5 mm spacing, 2,500 mm sheet, 114 mm parts | 1.5114 mm | 1.5162 mm |
| No spacing, 1,020 mm strip on a 990 mm sheet | 0.0039 mm | 0.0456 mm |
| No spacing, Blazewicz (7.5 units) | 0.00003 | 0.00032 |

A part with no spacing therefore needs about 0.08 mm more room per metre than
before to fit a sheet. Four tests that placed parts within 0.03 mm of the
sheet now allow that room.

A [report for jagua-rs](../../docs/upstream-drafts/jagua-offset-short-edges.md)
is drafted and not posted.

### Work clock

The six coefficients that measure collision-engine work were refitted on the
port's 58 jobs with separator work (20 s wall-clock runs, least squared log
error with a pull towards one common factor).

| Predicted/real search time per job | Minimum | Median | Maximum |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| v4 coefficients on `main` (1.1.0) | 0.40 | 0.97 | 1.78 |
| v4 coefficients on the port | 0.87 | 1.65 | 4.61 |
| v5 coefficients, the run they were fitted on | 0.46 | 0.95 | 1.96 |
| v5 coefficients, a later run of the final build | 0.38 | 1.03 | 2.21 |

Constructor checks keep their v4 value: in four 40-second repeatable runs the
warm start took 0.9 to 2.9 s against 2.6 to 2.9 s charged. Repeatable layouts
differ from v4 for the same job, seed and budget.

### Tests changed

- The four-pass `b-blaz1` construction reaches ten sheets on 14 of seeds 0 to
  19 on 2.0.0 (16 with sixteen passes) and on all 20 on 1.1.0, with no trial
  rejected. Its test now uses seed 1. In the 20-second search the same job
  ended at 9.66673 against 9.66858 on `main`.
- The run that reached an overlapping trial on 1.1.0 (`b-albano`, seed 1004,
  pass 75) no longer does. No rejected trial occurred in 640 seeds of 120
  passes, so its test keeps the run and checks that any rejected trial is
  neither accepted nor scored.
- The repair test's two source layouts were made at the 1.1.0 separation and
  no longer load. They are replaced by the constructor's layouts of the same
  jobs on 2.0.0.

All 554 workspace tests pass (two ignored), with strict Clippy, formatting,
rustdoc, both WebAssembly builds, the release CLI feature tests and the
notices and schema checks.

## Not measured

- Browser speed and quality (H4). The WebAssembly build compiles; its
  comparison with native has not been rerun.
- The common-line gate (H5, 234 layouts) and repeatable-mode material.
- Any held-out job. A new claim needs a new final run with the comparator on
  the same engine.
